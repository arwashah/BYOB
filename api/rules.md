## What an Ontario employee gets (and a solo worker doesn't)
[source: ontario-esa]
- Vacation: at least 2 weeks after each 12 months of work, with vacation pay of 4% of wages (3 weeks and 6% after 5 years).
- 9 paid public holidays: New Year's Day, Family Day, Good Friday, Victoria Day, Canada Day, Labour Day, Thanksgiving, Christmas Day, Boxing Day.
- 3 job-protected sick days a year (unpaid by law; many employers pay them). Employers can't require a doctor's note for them.
- Job-protected pregnancy leave (17 weeks) and parental leave (up to 63 weeks).
- Employer pays half of CPP contributions, an EI share, and workplace injury insurance (WSIB).
- Many employers also offer health and dental plans, disability insurance, life insurance, RRSP matching and counselling (EAP).

## EI special benefits for self-employed people
[source: ei-self-employed]
- Self-employed people can opt into EI special benefits by registering with Service Canada: maternity, parental, sickness, compassionate care and family caregiver benefits.
- They must wait 12 months from the date their registration is confirmed before they can apply for these benefits.
- They must have earned at least a minimum amount of net self-employment income in the year before they claim; the amount is set each year (check canada.ca for the current figure).
- They can cancel within 60 days of registering with no premiums owed. After that, if they never claimed, they can leave but pay premiums to the end of that year. Once they claim, they must keep paying EI premiums on self-employment income for as long as they are self-employed.
- Anyone expecting a baby, surgery or caregiving in the next 1-2 years should register now because of the 12-month wait.
- Quebec residents get maternity and parental benefits from the Quebec Parental Insurance Plan instead.

## CPP
[source: cpp]
- All self-employed workers pay both the employer and the employee portions of CPP, so they should set money aside for it at tax time.

## Health and dental
[source: dental]
- The Canadian Dental Care Plan (CDCP) helps people who: have no access to dental insurance (including through work, a pension, a purchased plan or a health spending account that covers dental), have an adjusted family net income under $90,000, filed their tax return for the previous year (and their spouse or partner did too), and are Canadian residents for tax purposes.
- Small businesses can often join group health plans through a chamber of commerce or industry association.

## Time off
[source: ontario-esa]
- Setting aside 4% of every payment matches what employers pay for vacation.

## Insurance
[source: wsib] for the WSIB line; [source: byob] for the rest
- Home insurance often excludes business equipment and business use.
- Equipment breakdown coverage is often an optional add-on to business property insurance, not included by default.
- Disability insurance replaces part of your income if you can't work because of illness or injury.
- In Ontario, sole proprietors, partners and independent operators can choose to buy WSIB optional insurance for themselves (it lasts at least 3 months and continues until cancelled). In most construction businesses, coverage is mandatory instead.
- BYOB explains and compares options. It never sells insurance and is not financial advice.

## Equipment
[source: byob]
- If one piece of equipment is the only way to earn money, it is a single point of failure: plan a repair fund and a backup (rented equipment, shared kitchen or studio, a group member).

## BYOB support groups (the pool)
[source: byob]
- Small groups of solo workers pledge gifts to each other. The app never holds money.
- Gifts are not guaranteed and are not insurance. There is a 3-month waiting period and a cap per claim.
